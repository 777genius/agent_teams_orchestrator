# Compare Vs GoClaw Gastown And Us

## Cortex vs GoClaw

### Where Cortex is stronger

- distributed-systems imagination
- OTP/runtime design taste
- mesh and gossip as explicit modes
- gateway plus sidecar story

### Where GoClaw is stronger

- durable engine maturity
- store-backed task lifecycle
- recovery tied to a stronger persisted center
- product breadth with more real operator/admin surface

## Cortex vs Gastown

### Where Cortex is stronger

- cleaner runtime decomposition
- much healthier substrate
- less architectural chaos
- better separation of ideas into subsystems

### Where Gastown is stronger

- raw orchestration imagination in some areas
- stronger provocation around roles, convoys, worktrees, and recovery concepts

### Practical verdict

Cortex is a much safer codebase to study than Gastown.
Gastown is more useful as a source of wild ideas.

## Cortex vs our stack

### Where Cortex is stronger

- OTP-style process architecture
- more explicit distributed-mode exploration
- cleaner separation of mesh/gossip ideas as dedicated runtime modes

### Where we are stronger

- deterministic bootstrap and operator control
- local coding-swarm ergonomics
- UI/cockpit fit for active human supervision
- more practical human-in-the-loop orchestration mindset

### Important nuance

If the question is:

- "who is more interesting as a distributed orchestration research object?"

then Cortex is stronger.

If the question is:

- "who is stronger today as a controlled coding-team orchestrator?"

then we are stronger.

## Short ranking by angle

- Distributed-systems imagination: `Cortex`
- Durable orchestration engine: `GoClaw`
- Wild concept mining: `Gastown`
- Human-controlled coding cockpit: `our stack`

## Main comparison insight

Cortex is the competitor to study when you want to ask:

"What if this were a proper concurrent/distributed runtime?"

GoClaw is the competitor to study when you want to ask:

"What does a more mature durable orchestration product look like?"
